Course Description
This course is an introductory programming course using the Python language. This course focuses on algorithm design, problem-solving strategies and program design. Topics covered include variables, types, expressions and control structures. Additional topics are standard input/output; file input/output; file streams; single and multi-dimensional arrays; searching; sorting; and recursion and its relation to iteration. This course also introduces object-oriented programming concepts such as classes and objects; syntax of class definitions; functions and parameter passing. F, S

Course Policies
This class is intended to help prepare you for a career that brings you joy.
Attendance Policy
Attendance will be taken regularly. If attendance will be a problem, please communicate with the instructor to identify solutions.


Life can be difficult. It is more difficult when you feel disconnected. We have regular meetings and fun interactions. I would like to help you feel checked in. Attendance will be taken at the start of every class. We will also be checking Canvas for the last activity. You are expected to be in class every day. You are also expected to log into Canvas every week to submit assignments, complete exams, or check for announcements. Perfect attendance (less than 3 absences and no skipped weeks in Canvas) will result in a one letter grade increase at the end of the semester. Six absences or two weeks of course inactivity will result in the reduction of a single letter grade, with an additional letter grade reduction after that for every week missed or additional 3 absences. If you miss three weeks or have 9 absences, the best you can hope for is a C. After four weeks of inactivity or 12 absences, the best grade you can earn is a D. Five weeks of course inactivity or 15 absences will be grounds for being withdrawn from the course. 


The broader discussion on attendance is that many students learn more from each other than they do from the course material. This is not possible without your presence in the discussions.


Last semester, many of my students gave me feedback about my attendance policy. There was significant support for trying a semester with the cameras on. Basically, friends told me that when they shut the cameras off, they stopped paying attention. And several friends told me that when they had classes that required the camera to be on, they stayed more engaged. I was against a camera policy. I wanted to leave this open because turning on the camera means different things for different people. For some of my friends, it is as simple as not playing on your phone in class. For others it might mean significant prep time for appearance, and possibly even more time to travel to a location with good internet, and then feeling drained for the rest of the day. I want to be flexible with people and treat people with respect to make good choices. I am willing to work with individuals on a case by case basis if we need to make exceptions. In general, I would like to have cameras on for all students, both in the classroom and out in TV land. Our classroom has a classroom camera, but it is not as good as your own web cam. But as long as you can get on Zoom and participate in the class, you can be anywhere you want. My classroom is open to you, but you are not required to be physically there.

 When you are trying to do this week’s work and then catch up on old work, you are going to be falling behind. Project 1


The big push this project is for you to really think through what you are going to do this semester to add something to your resume. If you can get it done in a single page, cool. If it takes you 50 pages, I understand. There is no example length. The good students that are really working on something cool usually have more than 2 pages, because they have lots of cool links that they want to work through that will help them with their project. The uninteresting projects can be done in less than a page.
This week you are making the plan. You are giving the things you will work through and spelling out when you will work through them. You are explaining why this matters to you. I would suggest you try to articulate why it matters to an employer as well.
For picking a project, there are no bad answers. If you do something you love, I would tell you to keep doing that. If you do something you hate, now you know to never do it again. If you pick something related to programming, you will have many friends in the class. If you do something outside of programming, you might have to look farther to find someone to work with you.
I left the requirements broad. This means that about anything you turn in I can count for full credit. The goal is to give you the freedom to choose something that will benefit you and your career. Unfortunately, in order to be able to accept a broad range of styles and career goals from all students, it is difficult to pin down what needs to be done. In the past, I have looked for reasons to give people full credit. I have accepted less than stellar work because they showed effort. This assignment is a pass fail grade, and I pass most anything. This assignment was never about a grade. Unfortunately, the grade is the only way I can get people to make sure they make time to do it. The push behind this is to help you do well on something you believe will benefit you.
The other thing that makes this a hard assignment is that not everyone has given thought to what their career should look like. It is hard to start to develop a skill set to support a career that does not exist.
I am sorry this is challenging. My goal is to help you become familiar with the big challenges of life, such as thinking about your future and owning the preparation process for it. It is my hope that as you become more familiar with these questions, you will be less stressed by them when the test of life comes your way.
Good luck friend. What you do with that freedom is up to you. I hope this helps.

* (5 points) Sentence: A single sentence describing what the question is asking

Academic Integrity
Academic integrity is expected and required. ALL suspected violations of the academic integrity policy will be reported. Sharing, copying, or doing work together is not permitted unless explicitly stated. Ignorance is not a defense against academic integrity violations. No forms of cheating or plagiarism will be tolerated. Plagiarism
An essential rule in any university class is that all printed and spoken work which a student submits should be entirely his/her own, or properly documented. If it is not, the student is guilty of plagiarism. The following are two types of plagiarism of which the student should be aware:
1. Word for word copying, without acknowledgement, of the language of another person. Obviously, having another person write or dictate all or part of one’s printed or oral work. In addition, a student should copy NO printed, spoken or electronic passage, no matter how brief, without acknowledging its source. This applies to even the briefest of phrases if they are truly individual and distinctive.
2. Another type of plagiarism is the unacknowledged paraphrasing of another’s ideas. A student should no more take credit for another’s thoughts than for another’s language. Any distinctively original idea taken from another should be credited to its author. If the student does not know whether another’s idea is distinctively original, he/she should incline to believe that it is; no fault attaches to over-acknowledgement, but under-acknowledgement is plagiarism.

Special Accommodations
Students requiring special accommodations due to a learning disability or physical limitation must notify the Dean of Students prior to the end of the first week of classes. The student should meet with each instructor after the first class period or during the instructor's office hours within the first week of class and discuss the specific accommodation options that have been recommended and how they will be adopted in his or her class. 
Students with Disabilities
If any member of the class feels that he/she has a disability and is in need of special accommodations, the instructor will work with you and Student Services in order to provide reasonable accommodations. This will help ensure that you have an equal opportunity to perform in this class. Please advise the instructor of such disability and the desired accommodations at some point before, during, or immediately after the first scheduled class period.
