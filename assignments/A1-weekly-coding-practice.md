# Weekly coding practice

## Purpose

Reinforce this week's technical concept with real, working code, and show
your process, not just your result. This is the "weekly topic
reinforcement" artifact named in `docs/course-ethos.md`, worth 25% of the
course grade (`docs/grading-model.md`).

## Reconciled 2026-07-27: this is fulfilled by the Coding Odyssey, not a separate problem set

Every technical week of the semester is a Coding Odyssey week. The
textbook's chapter for the week supplies the concept and the vocabulary —
it's still worth reading, and it's still part of the Monday lecture and
in-class conversation — but you don't practice it by solving standalone
chapter problems. You practice it by using that concept, that week, inside
your own persistent Odyssey world.

- **Weeks with a gate (2–5, 7–8, 10–13):** this assignment *is* that week's
  Odyssey Gate plus its open continuation. Do
  `assignments/odyssey_gates/week-NN.md` — nothing else is due under this
  name that week.
- **Checkpoint weeks (6, 9, 14, 16):** this assignment *is* that week's
  Coding Odyssey checkpoint. See `assignments/A2-coding-odyssey-project.md`.
- **Week 1, Week 15, Week 17:** no chapter-concept gate that week (Week 1 is
  universal across all five courses; Week 15 is a buffer/async week; Week
  17 is finals) — see each week's own `planning/week-NN.md` for what's
  actually due.

**This supersedes the previous framing of this file**, which described an
independent chapter-problem-set assignment running in parallel with the
Odyssey. If you're looking at a `planning/week-NN.md` that still describes
"chapter N practice" as a separate deliverable from that week's gate, the
gate is the current source of truth — flag the planning file for an update
rather than doing both.

## Do

1. If you want a concrete starting point before improvising your own
   scenario, work the **suggested textbook problem** listed in that week's
   gate file first — it's the same concept, phrased as a plain practice
   problem with one version per genre. This is optional scaffolding, not a
   second assignment: do it privately for practice, then let it become (or
   inform) your actual gate submission. Skip it entirely if you don't need
   it.
2. Do the current week's Odyssey Gate or checkpoint (see above for which).
3. Submit working code, representative output, and a short
   explanation/report of what it does and why — applying this week's
   textbook concept to your world, not in the abstract.
4. Demonstrate it in discussion or show-and-tell when scheduled.
5. Log it in your World Bible (`assignments/A2-coding-odyssey-project.md`).

## Grading — two axes, graded and reported separately

**Axis 1 — the working result (15 of 25 points).** For gate weeks: the
Quick Check (pass/fail floor) plus the Light Build continuation band
(Minimal/Solid/Strong — see `docs/curriculum/judgment_toolkit.md` §1–2),
mapped onto the historical effort ladder — gate not yet passed sits around
25%, gate passed with minimal continuation around 50–75%, gate passed with
a solid-to-strong continuation is 100%. For checkpoint weeks: the four-axis
Full Build rubric (`judgment_toolkit.md` §2) stands in for this axis
directly.

**Axis 2 — process (10 of 25 points).** Four dimensions, every week,
regardless of which vehicle fulfills Axis 1 — this axis is tracked
continuously, not tied to a specific chapter problem. Each scored 0
(absent) / 1 (present) / 2 (present and thoughtful), plus 2 points for the
whole package being organized and reusable:

| Dimension | What earns points | Evidence to submit |
|---|---|---|
| Rhetoric | Interrogated the question — restated it, challenged an assumption, or justified taking it as written, rather than silently answering as-is | 2–3 sentences at the top of the submission |
| Planning | A plan existed before code (outline, pseudocode, flowchart, or steps), and the submission notes where reality diverged from it | The plan artifact, however rough |
| Resource budgeting | Tracks every service/model/tier used and the limits hit, and explains why that tool fit the problem | A tool-and-limits ledger, a few lines |
| Knowledge management | Prompts and chats are tracked well enough to find and reuse next week | Prompt/chat log, attached or linked |

**Resource budgeting is graded on the tracking and reasoning, never on how
much was spent** — a student running only free/local tools can earn full
marks. Full detail and rationale: `docs/grading-model.md`.

## Support and integrity

Ask questions in class, office hours, or the shared class repository. AI is
a tutor and debugging aid, not a typist (`docs/course-ethos.md`) — you must
be able to explain and verify every line you submit, and you must credit
outside help or AI assistance in your submission (this feeds the rhetoric
and resource-budgeting dimensions above, it isn't a separate integrity
statement to append).
