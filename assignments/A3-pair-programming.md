# Paired programming and explanation

## Purpose

Practice explaining your reasoning out loud and reading someone else's
code, not just producing your own. Pair programming is the Wednesday active
work in this course's weekly rhythm (`docs/course-ethos.md`).

## Do

1. Work with a partner on the current week's problems (the same problems as
   `assignments/A1-weekly-coding-practice.md`, done together instead of
   solo). Take turns driving (typing) and navigating (reading, catching
   mistakes, asking questions); switch roles at least once per session.
2. If you share one repository, each partner commits their own work. Your
   pair's git log must show a lines-of-code split no more lopsided than
   80/20 — if one partner is consistently driving, that's a problem to fix
   before it's a grading problem.
3. Record, in your own words: the problem, your approach, what changed in
   the code, how you tested it, and specifically what *you* contributed
   versus your partner.
4. Note any outside help or AI/resource use — whose idea it was, and how
   you verified it.
5. Present your progress to your partner or the class when scheduled.

## What you submit: the paired-programming report

This is your own individual report, even if the code is shared — it's
graded on your documented contribution, not the pair's combined output
(`docs/grading-model.md`, 5% of the course grade). Include:

- The problem and your approach, briefly.
- What you personally wrote, fixed, or figured out (be specific — "helped
  with the loop" is not specific; "wrote the sentinel condition after our
  first version looped forever" is).
- A pointer to the commit(s) or lines that back that claim up.
- Anything you learned from watching or being corrected by your partner.

## Separately: the Friday feedback report

If your section does Friday show-and-tell peer feedback, that's graded
separately as its own 5% category — see
`assignments/A4-show-and-tell-reflection.md` and `docs/grading-model.md`.
Don't fold it into this report.

## Support and integrity

If schedule or enrollment makes pairing impractical some weeks, this format
may be dropped for that week — check with your instructor rather than
skipping silently. Ask questions in class or office hours if you're unsure
how to document a contribution split fairly.
