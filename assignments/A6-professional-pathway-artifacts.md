# Professional pathway artifacts

## Purpose

Build real career-planning evidence over the semester — an unofficial
transcript, degree plan, resume, target-role research, and online presence
— not a one-time generic worksheet. This is the career-connection strand of
the course (`docs/course-ethos.md`).

## Do — staged across three weeks, not one big submission

**Week 1 — establish baseline artifacts, ungraded** (largely covered by
`semester_kickoff_week`'s A01–A09 set; see that repo for the actual
handouts/rubrics once this course pulls from it directly):

- Unofficial transcript / degree audit.
- Advisor or progress check.
- A preliminary degree plan (it's fine if it's rough).
- A current resume, even incomplete.
- Current GitHub and LinkedIn profiles.
- An initial dream-job or target-role interest.

**Week 14 — update pass, graded on its own (5% of the course grade)**
(paired with that week's GitHub work; rubric:
`rubrics/A6-week-14-update_rubric.md`):

- Refresh your dream-job/target-role research.
- Do a skill-gap comparison against that role.
- Update your resume.
- Update GitHub and LinkedIn.
- Update your degree plan if anything's changed.
- **Changelog/diff entry:** write up what changed since your Week 1
  baseline and *why* — not just what you edited, but the reasoning behind
  each change (why the target role shifted, why you reworded a resume
  line, why you added a project). This is the artifact this update pass is
  really graded on: the reasoning trail, not just the refreshed content.

**Week 15 — completion and submission, graded on its own (5% of the
course grade)** (independent, asynchronous week — revise and organize what
you've already built rather than starting from scratch; rubric:
`rubrics/A6-week-15-submission_rubric.md`):

- Submit the full, completed set: transcript/degree audit, degree plan,
  resume, GitHub/LinkedIn, target-role research, skill-gap analysis.
- Add a brief reflection on your next steps — again, the reasoning behind
  each named step matters more than the list itself.

Week 16 does not carry this assignment — it's due in Week 15.

## Why this is graded the way it is

This is where students establish practices they can keep using as they
continue to grow in their profession — so both graded checkpoints reward
the *reasoning* behind an artifact (why this target role, why this skill
gap, why this change) over the artifact's polish. A rough resume with a
clear, specific rationale for each change should score higher than a
polished one with no visible reasoning behind it.

## Support and integrity

If you're unsure what "instructor-approved equivalent" looks like for any
artifact (e.g., you already have a finished degree plan from advising), ask
rather than redoing work you've already done elsewhere — bring what exists
and update it.
