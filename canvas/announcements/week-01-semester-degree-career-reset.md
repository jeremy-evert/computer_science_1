## Your life is the assignment this week.

I want you to spend some time answering four questions.

### 1. Are you enrolled in the right classes for this semester?

Look at your current schedule.

Do these classes actually move you toward where you are trying to go?

Do you need to add something?

Do you need to change something?

Is there a class on your schedule that should not be there?

I would like you to answer those questions for yourself **by noon Friday, August 21**, rather than waiting until the last possible minute. The university deadline to add a 16-week course is **Friday, August 21 at 4:30 p.m.** The refund-related drop/withdrawal deadline is **Friday, August 28 at 4:30 p.m.**

Those gates matter. I want you making the decision while you still have choices.

### 2. Do you understand the road between where you are today and graduation?

Pull up whatever applies to you:

- your unofficial transcript;
- your degree audit or progress report;
- your degree requirements;
- transfer work;
- prerequisite information;
- course rotations or sequencing.

Then try to lay out what remains **semester by semester**.

Look for the weird stuff.

Maybe one class requires another class first. Maybe something is offered only once a year. Maybe a transfer course never got applied correctly. Maybe you already completed something somewhere else. Maybe a substitution makes sense. Maybe there is some tiny academic trapdoor waiting three semesters down the road.

Finding that problem **now** is infinitely better than discovering it when you are trying to graduate.

And if you find something strange, bring it to somebody. Talk with me, your advisor, a department chair, Academic Records, or whoever needs to be part of the conversation.

### 3. Where are you actually trying to go?

Take out your résumé as it exists **today**.

Don't just proofread it.

Look at the person that piece of paper represents.

Then go find something that points toward your future. Find a real internship, job posting, graduate program, career, company, research area, certification, or role that makes you think:

**“I would love to be able to do that.”**

Now compare the two.

What separates the person represented by your résumé today from the person who could walk into that opportunity five or ten years from now?

Maybe it is knowledge, technical skills, classes, projects, internships, certifications, work experience, leadership, a portfolio, communication skills, people you need to meet, or something you have not discovered yet.

That gap is not an indictment of where you are.

**That gap is your map.**

### 4. Stitch the whole thing together.

This is what I am really asking you to do in the Wednesday work:

**This semester → Your degree or education → Your career → The life you want**

I am not asking you to produce a flawless 15-year prophecy.

I am asking you to make a **first draft of the map**.

Look at what you are spending your time on this semester. Look at where your education is taking you. Look at the opportunities you want someday.

Then ask:

**Does this road actually go there?**

If not, this is a remarkably good week to start turning the wheel.

### If you are still in high school

You may actually have an even bigger opportunity with this assignment because your map is still wide open.

Do not limit your investigation to one college or even assume that one particular path is automatically right for you.

Explore CareerTech programs, universities, community colleges and transfer pathways, certifications, apprenticeships, professional programs, and other ways people build careers.

Look at the paths people in your trusted circle have recommended, but don't stop there. Take what you discover back to family members, guidance counselors, teachers, advisors, classmates, mentors, and people already working in fields that interest you. Let them challenge your ideas and point out doors you didn't know existed.

Do not think about yourself as “just another high school student” who is eventually going to collect a diploma. Think about the person you might become **five, ten, or fifteen years from now.**

You are allowed to investigate serious opportunities. You are allowed to dream beyond the first obvious option.

Sometimes people miss wonderful futures not because they were incapable of reaching them, but because they never seriously considered them.

You do **not** have to know exactly what you want to do with the rest of your life this week. You do not have to pretend to be certain when you aren't. And I am not asking you to do something that makes you deeply uncomfortable.

What I **am** asking you to become comfortable doing is taking ownership of your future.

You have agency here.

Your professors, advisors, family, friends, counselors, and mentors can help you. We can point things out. We can open doors. We can warn you about potholes.

But ultimately, **I want you holding the pencil when your life gets sketched.**

I care about your assignments and I care about your grade. But I care much more about whether the semester you are about to spend your time, money, and energy completing actually moves you toward a life you want.

Sometimes those of us who care about people get overwhelmed and fail to deliver at the level those people deserve. That happened to me at the beginning of this week.

So we are going to recover from it.

Hopefully, we are also going to have some fun together this semester.

I'm flexible. I'm willing to listen. I'm willing to work with you. If this exercise uncovers a scheduling problem, prerequisite problem, transfer-credit problem, career question, or something else that you don't know how to solve, **bring it to me.**

We will figure out who needs to be in the conversation.

For Week 1, don't worry about having your whole life figured out.

**Just start drawing the map.**

Respectfully,

Jeremy
