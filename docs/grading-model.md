### 2026-08-14 — the Final reflection paper row was empty in Canvas (finding F01)

## Bonus mechanism (optional standalone practice)

**Decided 2026-08-12 (prompt 067).** Policy (unchanged, already decided):
optional, additive, never required, never a penalty for skipping, earned
credit is kept even after the normal Odyssey requirement is satisfied.

**Mechanism:** each of the 10 weekly-reinforcement weeks (2–5, 7–8, 10–13),
a student may submit **one** optional bonus-practice problem (instructor-
owned, a selected already-taught-aligned CS50P problem, or another vetted
open exercise — see `assignments/A1-weekly-coding-practice.md`) for **up to
5 flat bonus points**, added as a **0-points-possible extra-credit
assignment** inside the Weekly Reinforcement Assignment Canvas group.
This is Canvas's standard, well-understood extra-credit mechanism (an
assignment with `points_possible = 0` that a student can still be awarded
a positive score on): the group's percentage is `points earned / points
possible`, so any points scored on a 0-possible assignment are pure
addition to that group's percentage, with no risk of silently changing the
group's own scale for every other student.

**Cap:** counted at most 10 weeks across the semester (max 50 raw bonus
points). At the group's raw scale (25 points/week × 10 gate weeks = 250
raw points possible, plus the 3 checkpoints' own scale, over that group's
16.7-week span), 50 bonus points bounds the mechanism's total effect on
the final grade to roughly 3 percentage points — real, motivating, and
explicitly incapable of distorting the course grade or outcompeting the
value of doing the actual required work.

**One-sentence student-facing statement:** "Each reinforcement week you may
submit one optional bonus practice problem for up to 5 extra points added
to your Weekly Reinforcement Assignment grade, capped at 10 counted weeks
(50 points) for the whole semester."

Implemented in `course_foundry/cs1_desired_course.py`
(`_bonus_practice_grading`/`_weekly_bonus_object`) — see
`reports/014_cs1_grading_and_pre_savnac_closeout.md` for the Foundry-side
receipt.

(`https://bulldog.swosu.edu/publications/handbooks/student/semester-exam.php`):

scheduled." Cross-checked against the official Fall 2026 calendar
(`https://www.swosu.edu/calendar/files/2026-27-academic-calendar-unofficial.pdf`:
coursework resumes Nov 30, final exams Dec 7–11). For COMSC-1033-1415's

This was not previously caught: every weekly recurring category (Monday
Moment, Wacky Wednesday, Fun Friday, A3, A4, A7) was scheduled to push a
real graded Canvas object with a real due date in Week 16, directly
conflicting with the dead-days rule. **Smallest compliant fix (implemented
this pass):** Week 16 is exempted from every weekly recurring *graded*
category — extending the same "light, no new required deliverable" framing
Week 16 already carries for the Odyssey gate to the rest of the week's
categories too. Content (the Farkle/ML lesson, Wacky Wednesday/Fun Friday
sessions, the experience receipt) still happens; none of it is graded that
week. See `course_foundry/cs1_desired_course.py`'s `_DEAD_DAYS_WEEKS`/

implementation, and `reports/014_cs1_grading_and_pre_savnac_closeout.md`

## Automated-feedback / Marker pre-scoring boundary

**Decided 2026-08-12 (prompt 067).** Formalizes the existing
Harbor → Guidepost → Marker → Coach pipeline (`docs/course-ethos.md`) and
the existing mechanical-vs-human split
(`docs/curriculum/judgment_toolkit.md` §1: "some gates are mechanically
checkable... others need a human or a frontier-agent Quick Check") into one
practical, auditable workflow for the two-axis weekly rubric and the
Full Build checkpoints.

**What automated feedback (Marker) may inspect:** the student's submitted
code/text, the relevant gate's Quick Check criterion, and the applicable
rubric criteria text (two-axis weekly rubric, or the Full Build four-axis
rubric for checkpoints).

**What it may do:**
- Run the mechanically-checkable half of a Quick Check (does a required
  construct exist and run) as a hard pass/fail — no judgment call.
- Produce a **tentative pre-score** against the rubric's point scale
  (never a bare qualitative label — same "state explicit point anchors"
  discipline already used for the Monday Moment rubric,
  `cs1_desired_course.py`'s `_MONDAY_MOMENT_CRITERIA_BY_WEEK`), with a
  short evidence-cited rationale per criterion.
- **Flag, not guess,** whenever evidence is missing or ambiguous — the
  same refuse-rather-than-guess discipline already enforced in code
  (`PmRubricError`, `GateStatusError`): a Quick Check with no matching
  code, or a rubric criterion with nothing to cite, is reported as
  "unscoreable, needs human read," never silently defaulted to a mid-range
  score.

**What it must return (the receipt):** what was inspected, the mechanical
pass/fail result if applicable, the tentative pre-score per criterion with
its cited evidence, and an explicit flag list for anything ambiguous or
missing. This receipt **is** the "automated feedback out-of-office reply"
already promised in `docs/course-ethos.md` — the student sees it
immediately, labeled as a tentative pre-score, not a final grade.

**What remains professor-controlled:** the score that actually posts to
the gradebook. The professor's role is to review Marker's pre-score/flag
and enter the final number — not to re-derive it from scratch — consistent
with Jeremy's standing instruction that the human step should be entering
a score, not re-reviewing everything Marker already checked. The professor
retains sole authority over anything the pipeline flags as unscoreable,
and over the qualitative axes that are explicitly human/frontier-agent
judgment calls by design (Explanation, Demonstrability on the Full Build
rubric; the founding-charter and "is this actually a major noun in the
student's world" class of Quick Check named in `judgment_toolkit.md` §1).
Marker/Coach never posts a final grade autonomously.

## The two-axis rubric for the weekly reinforcement assignment

**Axis 1 — the working result (15 points of the 25).** For gate weeks
(2–5, 7–8, 10–13), this is that week's Odyssey Quick Check (pass/fail floor)
plus the Light Build continuation band, mapped onto the historical
effort-based ladder: gate not yet passed sits around 25% of the axis, gate
passed with minimal continuation 50–75%, gate passed with a solid-to-strong
continuation 100%. For checkpoint weeks (6, 9, 14), the four-axis Full
Build rubric (`docs/curriculum/judgment_toolkit.md` §2) stands in for this
axis directly instead of the ladder.

**Axis 2 — process (10 points of the 25).** Knowledge management and
resource tracking are part of the ethos, even in CS1. Four dimensions,
graded from what the student submits:

| Dimension | What earns points | Evidence submitted |
|---|---|---|
| **Rhetoric** | Interrogated the question — restated it, challenged an assumption, or justified taking it as written — rather than silently answering the prompt as-is | 2–3 sentences at the top of the submission |
| **Planning** | A plan existed before code: outline, pseudocode, flowchart, or steps — and the submission notes where reality diverged from it | The plan artifact, however rough |
| **Resource budgeting** | Tracks every service/model/tier used (free or paid), the limits hit (hourly/weekly/trial/free caps), and why that tool fit the problem — different models hit different. This is budget management as a skill. **Graded on the tracking and reasoning, never on how much was spent — a student running only free/local tools can earn full marks** | Tool-and-limits ledger, a few lines |
| **Knowledge management** | Prompts and chats are tracked: links, exports, or pasted excerpts, organized enough that the student could find and reuse them next week | Prompt/chat log attached or linked |

Scoring per dimension: 0 (absent) / 1 (present) / 2 (present and thoughtful),
× 4 dimensions = 8, plus 2 points for the whole package being organized and
reusable = 10.
