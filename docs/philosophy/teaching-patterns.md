# Teaching patterns visible in the archive

This is an evidence-based description of the archived course, not a proposed philosophy.

- **Practice first, with visible progress.** Weekly work is usually three chapter problems or a set of ZyBooks labs. Grading often rewards demonstrated effort and then the number of working solutions (25/50/75/100), with optional improvement bonuses.
- **Low-stakes sharing is part of learning.** Discussions mirror assignments, and “Show and Tell” asks students to demonstrate work, explain progress, and reflect. Pair programming in 2024–26 adds peer explanation and communication.
- **Creative application follows repetition.** Coding Odyssey / Choose Your Own Adventure Game checkpoints ask students to make a project of their choice, then revisit and extend it. Creativity, playability, clarity, and presentation are repeatedly valued.
- **Completion is supported but deadlines still teach pacing.** Syllabi describe two-week late-work windows, discretionary penalties, communication with the instructor, and occasional zero-point/bonus practice when time is short.
- **The instructor frames the class relationally.** Welcome videos, personal contact options, encouragement to ask for help, flexible attendance conversations, and end-of-course reflection recur. The tone is informal, humorous, and direct while emphasizing connection and persistence.
- **The course connects coding to a life beyond the class.** From 2023 forward, assignments ask students to inspect their degree progress, research a target job, build a resume/LinkedIn/GitHub presence, and identify skill gaps.
- **AI is treated as a tool that still requires understanding.** The 2025–26 materials explicitly pair programming with AI and require students to demonstrate that generated code works or identify what is wrong, with credit for resources used.

The archive does not show a stable conventional rubric or a stable textbook-only lecture pattern. The recurring assessment language is formative, pass/fail or effort-based, and demonstrative rather than highly standardized.
