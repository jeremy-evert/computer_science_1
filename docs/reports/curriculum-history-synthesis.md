# Curriculum history synthesis

## Scope and confidence

I reviewed all 12 archived Canvas JSON snapshots in 11 semester labels (spring 2021 contains two near-duplicate course IDs) and skimmed the uploaded files available in each semester. The JSON captures Canvas names, descriptions, discussions, pages, modules, quizzes, and file manifests; uploaded materials are sparse in most semesters, with the richest syllabus evidence in spring 2026.

## Durable backbone

The stable modern arc is Python foundations (printing/input, variables/expressions, types), branching, loops, functions, strings, Chapter 9 classes in two rounds, and Chapter 8 lists/dictionaries, usually with three Coding Odyssey/CYOAG checkpoints and a final creative project. Weekly chapter practice is the dominant assessment: three problems, working-code demonstrations, short reports/discussions, and a forgiving effort-to-working-solution scale. Show-and-tell, welcome/introduction work, and end-of-course reflection recur as community and metacognitive elements.

The older spring 2021 course is recognizably related but organized as four modules with chapter exams (1–10 and 12), discussion homework, show-and-tell, and a two-part Dream Job Project. Fall 2021 similarly uses fixed homework numbers and a CYOAG, while 2022 establishes the modern chapter/project vocabulary. From 2023 onward, career artifacts become a meaningful strand: transcript/progress, advisor and degree plan, resume, dream-job research, gap analysis, LinkedIn, GitHub, and learning/networking evidence.

## What evolved

Fall 2023–spring 2024 make the career pathway explicit and use Coding Odyssey names. Fall 2024 and fall/spring 2025 add repeated pair-programming-and-AI assignments for nearly every chapter, plus Git/GitHub work. Fall/spring 2025 also experiment with Coding Quests, a second parallel set of chapter practice, and one-off probability/real-world events such as Birthday and Monty Hall. Spring 2026 shifts to a detailed weekly page-based course launch, adds planning/mindfulness/time-management materials and a Show and Tell Journal, and includes optional Farkle/Q-learning practice; these are promising extensions but not yet long-term backbone.

## Pedagogy visible in the archive

The course is practice-heavy and demonstrative rather than exam-heavy: students show working code, explain it to peers, submit short reports, and can substitute a self-created problem. Grading language repeatedly rewards effort and incremental progress, while creative projects reward clarity, playability, and energy. Syllabus language emphasizes connection, communication, flexible help, attendance as participation, and a two-week late-work window with instructor discretion. The 2026 syllabus makes the same stance unusually explicit in humorous, personal language. AI is framed as permitted/useful only when students can verify, explain, and credit it.

## Inconsistencies and questions for Jeremy

- The archive contains no Canvas modules/pages for many semesters, and uploaded files are sparse; the apparent absence of material may reflect export limitations rather than what was taught.
- Chapter 8 is labeled Dictionaries in recent offerings, while older work includes lists and Chapter 8/9 conventions vary. Chapter 9 is consistently split into rounds, but exact concept boundaries are not always recoverable.
- Spring 2021 is duplicated under two course IDs with a duplicate Show and Tell entry in one copy. It also has a much more exam-oriented structure than the later course.
- Assignment counts rise sharply in 2024–25 because paired and Coding Quest copies coexist with the regular chapter assignments; this may represent intentional layered practice or Canvas duplication.
- The syllabus describes file I/O, arrays, searching, sorting, recursion, and object-oriented topics, but recurring Canvas evidence is strongest for the Python chapter arc and does not consistently show those other topics being assessed.
- A few zero-point or bonus items (CYOAG extensions, Coding Odyssey 3, Farkle/Q-learning, attendance events) show schedule-driven experimentation. Decide which should be retained as optional enrichment versus removed from the base course.
- Syllabus text changes materially in tone and policy detail between 2023 and 2026, especially attendance and late work. A current canonical policy should be chosen before templating future offerings.
