Reasoning Odyssey Gate — Week 10 — The Census

What are we learning? Lists — tracking a growing collection of named entities, built up over time, instead of one-off variables for each thing.

What am I building/changing? Your world's population/inventory/case-load/customer-base becomes enumerable: track a growing collection of named entities in a list, built up over more than one addition — not a list literal typed out once and never modified.

How does my world connect? Every entity you've been printing or checking individually since Week 2 can now live in one collection your loops and functions can operate over together.

What evidence do I keep? One World Bible line: what gate you passed, what broke.

Quick Check (pass/fail — all three required, no partial credit)

A list holds multiple distinct entities relevant to the world — not a list of unrelated scratch values.
The list is built up via .append() (or equivalent) at least twice across the program's run, not hardcoded as a single literal.
At least one operation traverses the list (a loop, len()-based report, or search) and reports something real about it.

Graded on two separate tracks, not averaged: this Quick Check (fully mechanical) plus a holistic Light Build pass — Strong/Solid/Minimal bands.

What exactly do I turn in? Your world code showing the list built incrementally and traversed, plus your World Bible entry. The suggested practice problem (append entities one at a time, then loop to report a roster/count) can directly be your gate submission.

What carries forward? Week 11 adds a dictionary that looks up an entity from this week's list by name or ID, replacing linear search by hand — plus your first Decide/Compare, defending that data-structure choice.
