Reasoning Odyssey Gate — Week 11 — The Registry, and the First Real Trade-off

What are we learning? Dictionaries — looking up an entity by key instead of searching a list by hand — plus your first Decide/Compare: committing to a data-structure choice in writing, then defending it against a real alternative.

What am I building/changing? Add a dictionary that looks up an entity from Week 10's list by name or ID — lookup replaces linear search by hand. Before building it, commit to your data-structure choice in writing, then compare it against at least one real alternative (why a dict here and not a list you search each time? would a set fit better somewhere else?).

How does my world connect? This directly extends Week 10's list — the same entities, now retrievable by key instead of found by scanning.

What evidence do I keep? One World Bible line: what gate you passed, what broke — plus your Decide/Compare choice and rationale, since it's part of your Judgment Log.

Quick Check (pass/fail — all three required, no partial credit)

A dict is used to look up an entity by a key (name/ID), not a manual loop-and-compare search.
The dict is populated from real world data (from the Week 10 list or equivalent), not a hardcoded literal.
At least one lookup is demonstrated returning a real result.

Decide/Compare #1 (separately graded)

What makes this pass: the comparison names a real trade-off (lookup speed vs. insertion order, memory, code complexity) specific to your world's actual data — not a generic "dicts are faster" restatement. The choice should visibly follow from the comparison, not precede it cosmetically.

Two separately-graded parts, not averaged: the Quick Check (fully mechanical) and Decide/Compare #1 (a real judgment call, graded on whether the trade-off is genuinely yours, not textbook).

What exactly do I turn in? Your world code with the dict-based lookup working, your written Decide/Compare commitment-then-comparison, and your World Bible entry logging both.

What carries forward? Week 12 starts turning your tracked entities into real classes — the major noun you've been storing as dict/list entries since Weeks 10–11 gets a constructor and its own methods.
