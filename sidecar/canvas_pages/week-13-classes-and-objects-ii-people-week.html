Reasoning Odyssey Gate — Week 13 — The World Becomes Real Things (Round 2)

What are we learning? Classes and objects, continued — making two of your world's classes actually interact, plus your capstone Decide/Compare: a bigger version of Week 11's move, applied to the whole world instead of one data structure.

What am I building/changing? At least two of your world's classes must interact — a method call between objects, not just standalone instances that never talk to each other. If Week 12 only produced one class, this is where a second one (or a second instance type) shows up and the two connect. Also: pick one real open design decision in your world, commit to a choice in writing, then compare it against a real alternative and state what changed your mind (or confirmed your choice).

How does my world connect? This directly extends Week 12's single class into two classes that actually talk to each other — the natural next step of the same refactor.

What evidence do I keep? Log the gate result and the Decide/Compare capstone choice/rationale in your World Bible — this feeds directly into Week 14's Full Trail Debrief and the Week 17 final Judgment Log review.

Quick Check (pass/fail — all three required, no partial credit)

At least 2 distinct classes exist (or a second meaningfully different instance role of the same class, if the genre only naturally supports one noun-type).
At least one method call crosses between two objects — object A's method calls or reads from object B, not two isolated silos.
The interaction produces a real, demonstrable effect — a state change, a computed result — not just proof the syntax runs.

Decide/Compare #2 (capstone — separately graded)

Applied to the whole world, not one data structure. Commit to the choice before comparing; name the actual trade-off for this world at this stage of the semester, not a textbook trade-off; state why the comparison — not the alternative — is what changed or confirmed your mind.

Two separately-graded parts, not averaged: the Quick Check (fully mechanical) and the Decide/Compare capstone (judgment-graded).

What exactly do I turn in? Your world code with two classes interacting, your written capstone Decide/Compare, and your World Bible entry logging both.

What carries forward? Week 14's Full Trail Debrief asks specifically what broke in this OOP refactor and what you'd design differently starting fresh — start taking notes now, while it's fresh.
