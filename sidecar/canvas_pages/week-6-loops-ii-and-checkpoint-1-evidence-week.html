Reasoning Odyssey Checkpoint 1 — Week 6 — The Baby Project

What are we learning? No new technical concept — this week consolidates everything taught through loops (variables/types, branching, loops) into the submit/explain/demonstrate/reflect rhythm you'll reuse at every future checkpoint.

What am I building/changing? Submit a working "baby project" version of your world — small, complete, low-stakes. A few dozen lines is completely acceptable. Whatever loop-driven behavior you built for Week 5's gate is a fully acceptable core — extend it lightly (one more piece of state, one more branch) rather than starting over.

How does my world connect? This checkpoint looks back across Weeks 2–5: the founding charter, the first decision, and now a loop that changes things over time, all working together in one run.

What evidence do I keep? One World Bible line: what checkpoint you passed, what broke, what's still rough.

Success criteria (25 points — two-axis checkpoint rubric)

AxisPointsWhat it measures
Working result15Checkpoint not yet passed (nothing new since Week 2) sits near 25%; a minimal baby project 50–75%; a solid, genuinely explained-and-demonstrated baby project 100%.
Process10Rhetoric, planning, resource budgeting, knowledge management (2 pts each) + 2 pts for an organized, reusable package.

Small scope is not penalized — a missing explain/demonstrate step, or a project that doesn't actually run, is what costs points here.

What exactly do I turn in? Four things: (1) submit a working version that runs end to end, (2) explain in your own words what it does and what changed since Week 2, (3) demonstrate it runs — a short transcript of real output is enough, no video required, (4) reflect on one choice or revision that made your earlier work fit together better.

What carries forward? Week 7 starts full graded functions instruction — you'll pull one action your baby project already does into its own reusable function.
