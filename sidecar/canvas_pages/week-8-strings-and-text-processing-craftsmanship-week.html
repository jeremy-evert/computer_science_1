Reasoning Odyssey Gate — Week 8 — The World Starts Reading Its Own Mail

What are we learning? Strings and text processing — using real string operations (indexing/slicing, search, or transform methods) to pull information out of free text, instead of just formatting text for display.

What am I building/changing? Parse or transform a piece of free-text input in your world — a log entry, a witness statement, a customer message, a ship's log line — using real string operations. print(f"...") interpolation alone doesn't count as processing.

How does my world connect? The function you built in Week 7 is a natural home for this week's parsing logic — a function that takes raw text and returns the extracted piece of information, continuing last week's refactor habit.

What evidence do I keep? One World Bible line: what gate you passed, what broke.

Quick Check (pass/fail — all three required, no partial credit)

Input text is searched, sliced, or transformed using real string methods (.split(), .find(), slicing, .strip(), etc.) — not just formatted for display.
The result of that processing is used for something — a decision, a stored value — not processed and immediately discarded.
At least one input-validation or whitespace/edge case is handled on purpose.

Graded on two separate tracks, not averaged: this Quick Check plus a holistic Light Build pass — Strong/Solid/Minimal bands. The first two criteria are mechanically checkable; the edge-case criterion needs a light human read for whether it was deliberate.

What exactly do I turn in? Your world code showing the string processing and its result being used, plus your World Bible entry. The suggested practice problem (parse a made-up log line for one specific piece of data) can directly be your gate submission.

What carries forward? Week 9 (strings continued) is Reasoning Odyssey Checkpoint 2 — your first real project pass, not a dry run — graded with the full Build rubric this time, not a Quick Check.
