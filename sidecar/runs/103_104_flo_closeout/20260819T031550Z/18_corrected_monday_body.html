Good habits, not talent

What actually makes people successful in a demanding course isn't raw talent — it's a small set of habits:

  1. Small steps, tested often. Don't write (or plan, or build) a huge amount before checking your work. Check constantly.
  2. A clear debugging story: Expected → Actual → Tried. When something goes wrong — in code, in a proof, in a project, in life — you can name what you expected, what actually happened, and what you already tried. That's the difference between a bad help request and a good one: - Bad: "It doesn't work." - Good: "I expected X, I got Y, here's what I tried."
  3. Evidence over vibes. Screenshots, error messages, specific examples — not just a feeling that something is wrong.

If your own "debugging story" (about anything — tech or not) involved trying more than one thing before it worked, you're already doing this. That instinct is the whole skill; the rest is practice.
